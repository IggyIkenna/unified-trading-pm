Strategy carve-out for POD architecture, hand-over boundary and acceptance

This document defines a credible standalone hand-over of the contracted strategy into a POD segregated portfolio: the components transferred and the form each takes, the runtime path through them, the interfaces at which platform capabilities are substituted, and the acceptance test that says the hand-over is complete. It is written to be planned and contracted against.

Working principle

The hand-over transfers a strategy that POD's engineering team can make operational inside a segregated portfolio. It does not transfer a replica of the operating platform built around strategies, and it does not need to, because POD already operates that layer. The boundary is made explicit in code: strategy decision logic is portable, and every point where a capability is supplied from outside the strategy appears as a typed interface rather than as something silently absent.

01

The counterparty, the contract, and the scope it sets

POD is the entity that would operate the carved system, inside one of its segregated portfolios. The consulting agreement between Elysium AM Ltd and IkeNova Ltd, executed on 3 March 2025 and signed for Elysium by Patrick Lynch, is the contract the work originated under and remains the basis on which this hand-over is scoped. The boundary described in this document is not a commercial preference expressed after the fact. It follows from that agreement and from what POD already operates.

The operatorPOD, a regulated fund operating system administered under the Central Bank of Ireland, running segregated portfolios inside an SPC, one sub-fund per portfolio manager
The contractAnnex A already lists the excluded scope; Article 4 already sets the ownership test. Both predate this document
The intentA system POD can run today and layer its own risk management on top of, rather than a system that needs the rest of a platform underneath it
Where the boundary comes from, and what falls on each side of it

Three reasons the boundary sits where it does

First, the agreement already excludes the fast and clever parts. Annex A lists, under Scope Exclusions: tokenisation of fund, fund setup and administration, fund settlement and clearing, prime broker and exchange relationship management, ultra low latency execution, liquidity provision, and post-launch maintenance and upgrades, the last of these noted as arrangeable separately. Excluding smart and fast execution is therefore the agreed scope of the engagement rather than a restriction introduced at hand-over.

Second, Article 4 sets the ownership test. All Work Product developed under the engagement is works made for hire and the exclusive property of the Elysium group, with an irrevocable assignment of anything that cannot be classified that way. What the agreement reserves to the Consultant is generic programming methods and open-sourced components. The line therefore falls between what was developed under this engagement and independent platform IP built before and beside it. The basis and staked-basis strategy sits on POD's side of that line and transfers. The multi-asset-group platform, its smart execution layer and the other archetypes sit on ours and do not.

Third, POD already owns the layer that is excluded. Fund administration, NAV, fee calculation and fund accounting run on-chain inside POD. Custody runs through Ceffu, Copper and Coinbase Prime. Venue connectivity runs through Haruko at more than 140 venues. Excluding our reconciliation engine, our wallet transfer path and our treasury layer is a decision not to duplicate infrastructure POD already operates and already trusts, rather than capability withheld. POD's own positioning is that trading IP is owned entirely by the trading teams and portfolio managers, and this hand-over is drawn to match that.

What the restriction is for

Each exclusion above makes the delivered system smaller, more legible and easier to take responsibility for. A strategy that reads a frozen configuration at startup, places limit orders, halts on a local hard limit and reports its state to your own reconciliation and NAV layer is a system POD can run today and own the risk of. A strategy carrying its own dynamic universe rotation, its own execution optimiser, its own treasury path and its own kill-switch protocol would arrive with a second operating model attached to it, competing with the one POD already runs.

02

What "runnable" means, the acceptance bar

"Runnable" is defined narrowly and deliberately. It means your team can take the package, supply credentials and infrastructure, complete a clearly identified set of substitutions, and run the contracted strategy against the agreed venues and instruments inside a POD segregated portfolio, with POD's administrator, custody and NAV layer sitting where the platform's own post-trade services would otherwise sit. It does not mean inheriting an operating model.

Out of scopeSmart execution, dynamic universe and allocation, reconciliation and attribution, treasury movement, version governance, multi-client machinery

The two paths

This document specifies one of two paths. They differ as follows; the election point and support period are defined in the service-level agreement rather than here.

  • Odum-operated. The strategy runs on our platform as a client-isolated deployment mapped to a POD segregated portfolio. POD holds read access to its own positions, runs, attribution and strategy decision records, queryable remotely. The ten interfaces in §05 resolve to maintained services.
  • POD-operated. The package in §03 transfers. The ten interfaces resolve to POD services, local implementations or static values, and §08 lists the standing operational functions that come with running it, most of which POD already discharges for every sub-fund it hosts.
Status of this specification

The eleven packages in §03 are a proposed extraction structure, not existing repositories. The strategy, adapter and contract code they contain is production code running today; the packaging, the interface layer and the local implementations are scoped extraction work. §07 is the acceptance test that would close it.

Inspection is not transfer, and the two have different scopes. Separately from this specification, the complete strategy service is being shared for inspection, which is broader than the package a carve-out would transfer, because it is the whole repository rather than an extraction from it. This document describes what a self-run hand-over would contain; it does not describe the limits of what is made available to read. Where the two differ, the difference is deliberate.

Three notes on the table. First, no package is a directory copy: each is an import closure taken over the repository tier graph, which is computable exactly because imports only ever point downward through that graph, so the package is reproducible from a commit rather than assembled by hand. Second, the count of components is larger than the count of source repositories, because the extraction splits along capability lines rather than along our internal repository boundaries. §10 maps it the other way, from the estate to the package.

Third, there is no custody package. Our own custody integrations are Cloud KMS held keys and Copper, and Copper is one of the three providers POD already uses. Rather than ship a second custody abstraction alongside POD's Ceffu, Copper and Coinbase Prime arrangements, the package carries only the narrow signing path the on-chain staking leg needs. Everything else about where assets sit and how they move resolves at the treasury contract in §05.

Runtime path through the carved system

The runtime path is short and auditable by design. Seven steps from a venue event to a persisted decision, each owned by exactly one component.

Seven stepsEvent, normalise, decide, guard, order intent, place, persist. One owner each
One decision pointAll strategy logic sits in step 3; the components either side are transport and safety
AuditableA reviewer can follow one tick end to end without entering platform code
The seven steps, their owners, and where a capability is supplied from outside

The design intent is that every place where a capability is supplied from outside the strategy is an explicit interface boundary rather than something embedded invisibly in the carved code. That gives a clean engineering choice at each seam: point it at a service POD already runs, implement it locally, or resolve it to a maintained Odum service.

06

Configuration and parameter snapshot

At the hand-over point the package receives a configuration snapshot, read once when the process starts. Several values that the configuration format presents as constants are, on the platform, outputs of services that recompute them, and the file format does not distinguish the two cases, so it is stated here explicitly.

TransferredOne parameter set, a documented schema with validation constraints, the covered universe, and a version identifier binding source, config and fixtures
Not transferredParameter history, the search space, the optimisation pipeline, auto-tuning, alternate variants, research notebooks, hot reload
ConsequenceThe set is correct for the conditions it was fitted against and does not re-derive itself
What the snapshot contains, and which inputs are constants here but services on the platform

The snapshot comprises exactly five things:

  • One parameter set, appropriate to the agreed universe at the agreed version and date.
  • A human-readable schema describing every strategy parameter and its validation constraints.
  • The exact venue and instrument universe covered by the acceptance test in §07.
  • A version identifier tying source, configuration and test fixtures together.
  • The replay fixtures the acceptance test runs against.

It does not include the parameter history, the search space, the optimisation pipeline, an auto-tuning loop, alternate strategy variants, or research notebooks, unless separately agreed. Nor does it include a reload path: changing a parameter means editing the configuration and restarting the process, which is a deliberate simplification rather than an omission. It means the configuration a run started with is the configuration it ran on, and that is a property worth having when the run has to be explained to an administrator afterwards.

Whether a frozen parameter set drifts materially over a given horizon is a question about the strategy's environment rather than about the software, and it is measurable directly: compare its behaviour against a re-derived one over the same window. It is raised here only because it is not visible from reading the package.

The hand-over is complete when these nine conditions hold on POD infrastructure, against a POD segregated portfolio. They are written to be executed and signed off rather than agreed in principle, and the last one is the most important.

The nine acceptance conditions
Acceptance test Executed jointly during the support period defined in the SLA
# Area Acceptance condition
01 Build The package set builds from a clean checkout using documented dependencies, on POD infrastructure, without access to our estate
02 Connectivity Connects to each agreed venue in sandbox or an agreed safe production mode, authenticating with the segregated portfolio's own credentials
03 Market state Consumes the required market and account events and constructs the minimal strategy state from them
04 Decision logic Produces deterministic, identical strategy decisions for the supplied replay fixtures
05 Orders Expresses strategy order intents as limit orders through the basic execution implementation, including the multi-leg sequence the structure requires
06 Safety Local hard limits and the local halt are demonstrated to fire under induced conditions, and the halt is observable to POD's own risk framework
07 Configuration Runs on the frozen agreed configuration, and fails loudly and clearly on an invalid one

09 Documentation Your engineering team can enumerate every external dependency it must resolve to operate independently: the ten contracts in §05 and the nine functions in §08, with the POD service that answers each one named

Condition 9 carries the most weight because it is the one that prevents a hand-over from looking complete and failing later. A package that builds, connects and trades in a sandbox but leaves its substitution list implicit is the failure mode worth designing against, which is why the interface seam exists as a shipped artefact rather than as an appendix to a document. In a POD deployment most of that list resolves to something POD already runs, which is precisely why it is worth enumerating rather than assuming.

Two structural notes for planning. First, four of the nine functions are the fund operating layer rather than the trading layer: reconciliation, independent marking, reporting and data retention are exactly what POD is built to provide, which is why the corresponding capabilities are excluded from the package rather than duplicated inside it. Second, of the nine, seven are insensitive to how many strategies they serve. A release pipeline, a monitoring estate and an on-call rota cost substantially the same for one strategy as for several. That is why the operational estate rather than the strategy code tends to dominate the cost of a self-run deployment, and it is the part most usefully estimated against your own infrastructure rather than from this document.

Two numbers about decentralised venues that measure different things

Fifty-nine decentralised protocols are covered as data sources, which is what the research and backtest surface reads and what a strategy can be developed against. Three protocol families are wired end to end for live on-chain instruction dispatch: Uniswap for swaps, AAVE V3 for supply, withdraw, borrow and repay, and Lido for staking and unstaking. Those are different measurements and the second is the one that matters to a live book. For this hand-over it is the relevant one too, because Lido is the leg the staked-basis structure uses.

Regulated venues as the worked discontinuity

A regulated futures venue is not another venue with a different symbol format. It brings a different protocol family rather than REST and WebSocket, a clearing relationship to establish and maintain, regulatory reporting obligations, a margin model computed by the clearing house rather than from a collateral haircut, and cash settlement on a settlement cycle rather than a wallet transfer. Each touches a different layer.

On the platform, part of that groundwork exists: a broker-gateway service managing the long-lived connection to a regulated broker, a TradFi market-data path with its own vendor integration and instrument definitions for futures and options, a pricing service already computing greeks and carry-family rates, and a delta-one feature family. Adding a regulated venue is an integration project with a compliance workstream beside it. POD-operated, the same ambition is a futures-capable trading system built next to the crypto one, then reconciled with it.

On the shared spine

Every strategy on the platform emits the same instruction contract and writes the same ledgers, so an improvement to execution, reconciliation or a venue integration applies to all of them. A transferred package is a snapshot at a commit and does not receive later platform work. That is a property of any code transfer, stated here so it is priced rather than discovered.

What transfers well, and what transfers poorlyReference
  • Transfers cleanly: the decision logic, the venue connectivity, the type system and the ledger writers. Readable, statically typed, self-contained at the decision level.
  • Transfers as a snapshot: reference data, collateral parameters and the fitted configuration. Correct on the day; each needs an owner and a refresh path afterwards.
  • Does not transfer: anything whose value is in being continuously produced: captured history, coverage accounting, independent marks, the determinism verdict, and later platform work.
