Section 1

The proposition

DART is Odum Research's institutional capital-markets technology platform. It provides a reusable infrastructure layer for quantitative research, large-scale data processing, strategy simulation, deployment, execution and production monitoring — one codebase, one operating surface, spanning digital assets, traditional markets, sports and prediction markets.

It is a platform, not a consulting engagement. The architecture is deliberately multi-tenant: a common core serves many institutional users and many strategies, rather than a bespoke build per client. Onboarding an institutional user configures the platform; it does not fork it. That distinction is the whole commercial thesis, and it is why the consumption curve in section 5 compounds with users rather than scaling linearly with headcount.

Odum Research Ltd is an FCA-authorised UK investment firm (firm reference 975797, authorised since 2023). DART is built for professional and institutional market participants. The authorisation covers Odum's regulated activities as a firm; it is not an endorsement of any particular technology, venue or counterparty, and nothing here should be read as one.

Google Cloud is the infrastructure layer underneath all of it. The 112 TB corpus, the research and simulation environment, the always-on execution spine and the reporting surface all run here. What we are asking Google to support is not an experiment looking for a use case. It is the scaling of a platform that already exists, already runs, and is already regulated.

Why consumption expands from here

Four things multiply together. More datasets — traditional markets alone quadruples as CME futures and options land. More strategies under research and in production. More simulation per strategy, as the research universe widens from a narrow sample to the full captured corpus. And more institutional users, each mandate running in its own isolated process tree because capital isolation is not optional. An increasingly capable AI and agent layer sits across all four.

The ask, stated plainly

We are proposing a bespoke three-year arrangement on a tapering discount: 80% in year one, 50% in year two, 30% in year three. Year one is $300,000 of gross consumption — $25,000 a month, of which we fund $5,000. The taper is the point: our share rises every year as the platform scales, and by year three we are paying the large majority of a much larger bill.

Across the term that is $2.0M of gross consumption, $870,000 of support, and $1.13M paid to Google in cash. Google's annual credit exposure barely moves — $240k, $300k, $330k — while our cash payment grows nearly thirteenfold. Section 5 sets out the arithmetic.

The $25,000 starting point is not an aspiration we are negotiating up to. August 2026 is already tracking near $18,000 a month across Google Cloud and AWS combined, against effectively nothing invoiced a year ago, and the workload behind it is still expanding. It is the next step on a curve that has climbed all year — and our own modelled demand for those same twelve months is materially higher than $300,000.

Without support, the honest answer is that consumption goes the other way. We would keep Google Cloud for bulk data storage — roughly $1,000 a month — and split compute, model inference and CI across AWS and Azure on price. We would rather not. Running one regulated platform across three providers is an operational headache we would be taking on for no reason other than cost. But at our current scale it would be the responsible call, and we would make it.

DART is one operating surface. A strategy moves from raw market data through research and simulation into live execution without changing systems, and one monitoring, reporting and governance layer covers every stage.

Three deployment configurations of one platform

These are configurations, not bespoke builds — the same core, with different parts of it exposed. They matter to the forecast because each consumes infrastructure differently and they compound rather than substitute. A user on signals-in drives execution, reconciliation and reporting load. A user on the full workflow additionally drives research and simulation load, which is by far the larger number.

Mode 01 · Client → Odum
Client-provided signals
Research stays with the client's team and their signal logic is never exposed to us. Odum runs execution, reconciliation, monitoring and reporting.
Mode 02 · Research → execution
Full workflow
The whole stack: granular tick, book, on-chain and alternative data; the research environment; the promotion ladder; live execution with post-trade analytics.
Mode 03 · Odum → counterparty
Odum-provided signals
Signals delivered under agreed scope; execution stays with the counterparty. Delivery health and acknowledgement flows run through DART.

Why this scales commercially

  • One infrastructure base, many mandates. The marginal cost of the next client is compute and storage, not a rebuild.
  • Clean IP boundaries. Clients keep their signal logic; we run everything around it. That widens the addressable set to teams who would never outsource research.
  • Regulated wrapper. FCA authorisation lets deployments stand up to allocator and regulator scrutiny — a real barrier to entry, and slow to replicate.
  • We run on it ourselves. The platform trades our own capital, so the research and execution load exists whether or not the next user onboards.
  • Data gravity compounds it. Market data, derived features, simulation results and strategy metadata all accumulate in one place. Every subsequent research and AI workload runs next to that corpus — which is what makes the estate sticky rather than portable.

Section 3

Why consumption grows

Every line in the forecast belongs to one of four workload families. Each has a distinct scaling driver, and they compound: more data widens research, more research produces more strategies, more strategies mean more production footprint, and the AI layer sits across all three.

Workload family Scaling driver Google Cloud consumption
Data More markets, deeper history, more features, client-specific datasets. Cloud Storage; Compute Engine and Cloud Run for capture and transformation.
Research & simulation Strategy archetypes × runs per day × universe width. The dominant multiplier. Compute Engine (Spot), Cloud Run jobs, Batch; heavy Cloud Storage reads.
Production trading Venues, live strategies and mandates — one isolated process tree per client. Always-on Cloud Run, Pub/Sub, Memorystore, Cloud SQL, networking.
AI & agents Engineering throughput today; operational and client-facing intelligence next. Vertex AI and Model Garden inference, accelerators, orchestration compute.

Data

112 TB across 79 buckets today. Cost tracks pipeline throughput as much as corpus size: roughly 48% object operations, 30% stored bytes, 22% retrieval — so it rises with research reads as well as with ingest. Historical backfill is a finite job that completes; what persists is the monthly increment, roughly 2% of the corpus, plus live streaming of a similar volume again. Step changes come from onboarding new sources, and the traditional-markets expansion is the large one in this window.

Research & simulation

This is the largest growth line and the one with the steepest curve. The shape is simple: ten backtests per strategy archetype per day across the full history, approximately one thousand backtests to take a single strategy from idea to live, and one T+1 batch-versus-live reconciliation run per live archetype per day. Archetype count and universe width multiply together, and both are increasing. Unit economics are in appendix B.

Production trading

The always-on spine — event transport, execution, strategy, risk and live market data — plus continuous paper trading alongside every live strategy, so that live performance can be tracked against backtest on a T+1 basis. Because capital isolation is a regulatory and commercial requirement, each client runs in its own process tree; this line therefore scales with mandates, not only with strategies. Colocated execution in additional regions is the other multiplier.

AI & agents

Today this is an engineering and operations layer: agents implement and test strategy code, diagnose pipeline failures, investigate production incidents, and retrieve across our documentation and data estate. The workload is real and it is large — we measured 296 billion tokens a month against our own infrastructure, detailed in appendix A. None of it currently runs on Google Cloud, and migrating it onto Vertex AI and Model Garden is the live decision this forecast assumes.

Framed properly, this is not a productivity story. Odum already operates an agentic quantitative research environment at scale — agents retrieve datasets, generate experiment and strategy code, orchestrate simulations, analyse results and diagnose pipeline failures, hundreds of tasks a day. What is internal today is the user, not the capability. The product step is exposing that environment to institutional researchers on the full DART workflow, so they can move from hypothesis to data retrieval to strategy specification to simulation to analysis conversationally.

We should be clear about the boundary. The agentic layer runs in production today against our own research and engineering. The client-facing surface is roadmap. We would rather say that plainly than overstate it.

Where the AI layer stops

This matters more in our domain than most. Systematic trading is a high-consequence environment, and the architecture is explicit about what a model is allowed to touch. No language model reaches capital. The agentic layer proposes; deterministic systems dispose.

Layer 01 — probabilistic
Agentic research and operations
Dataset interrogation · experiment design · strategy and feature code generation · simulation orchestration · result analysis · operational diagnostics and incident investigation. Everything here is a proposal.
Layer 02 — gate
Validation and approval
Automated tests · backtests across the full captured history · paper trading alongside live · the promotion ladder · risk validation · human approval. Nothing advances to production without clearing this layer, and the ladder is deliberately expensive — roughly a thousand backtests to promote one strategy.
Layer 03 — deterministic
Production systems
Order execution · pre-trade risk limits · position and capacity limits · drawdown controls · per-client capital isolation · T+1 reconciliation · kill switches. Conventional, auditable, deterministic code. No model inference sits in this path.

Why this is worth stating to an infrastructure partner. It explains why our Vertex consumption is large and growing without that consumption representing unsupervised automated decision-making over client capital. The AI layer accelerates research and operations; the controls that govern money are deterministic, human-gated and independently auditable — which is what an FCA-authorised firm is required to be able to demonstrate.

Section 5

The case for Google

A credit is an investment, so the relevant question is not whether Odum needs it. It is whether the consumption it creates persists after it expires. We think the structure here is unusually good on that test, for one specific reason: the workload is not created by the credit. It already exists and is already growing. The credit determines where it runs.

The proposal: support that tapers as we scale

We are not asking for a flat discount that expires at a cliff edge. We are proposing a three-year taper — 80%, then 50%, then 30% — deliberately structured so that our share of the bill rises every year while the bill itself grows.

Proposed three-year structure. Gross consumption in USD; Year 1 is the forecast in section 4; Years 2 and 3 are planning figures.
Term Gross consumption Discount Google support Paid to Google
The two numbers that matter Google's annual support barely moves: $240k, $300k, $330k. Odum's annual cash payment grows from $60,000 to $770,000 — nearly thirteenfold. By year three Google is collecting more in cash from us in a single year than it extended in support across the entire first two. That is the whole design: the support does not scale with the relationship, the revenue does.

Years 2 and 3 are planning figures, not commitments, and we would rather say so than dress them up. What we can say with confidence is the direction: our modelled demand for year one alone is roughly $780,000 unconstrained (appendix D) against the $300,000 we are committing to. The constraint on years 2 and 3 is not whether the workload exists.

Three things that make this durable

  • The consumption is load-bearing, not experimental. This is a regulated firm's production trading infrastructure. Migrating it away once it is running live is expensive and risky in a way that moving a research prototype is not — which cuts both ways, and is exactly why the platform decision is being made now rather than later.
  • Demand already exceeds the ask. We are not projecting growth into a credit and hoping it materialises. We are deferring work today for budget reasons. A credit converts deferred work into consumption immediately.
  • Client growth compounds the estate. Each mandate adds an isolated production footprint, its own research load and its own reporting surface. The platform's unit of growth is a customer, and every one of them lands on Google Cloud.
The alternative, without editorial If support is not available, we reduce Google Cloud to bulk storage — roughly $1,000 a month — and split compute, inference and CI across AWS and Azure on price. We would rather not. Operating one regulated platform across three providers costs engineering time we would rather spend on the product, and we would be doing it purely for cost reasons. But at our current scale it would be the responsible decision.
Section 6

Why Google Cloud specifically

Data
Cloud Storage handles a 112 TB Hive-partitioned Parquet corpus with the throughput characteristics our pipeline needs, and BigQuery sits naturally alongside it for analytics and client reporting without moving the lake.
Compute
Spot Compute Engine, Cloud Run jobs and Batch fit research and simulation well: the workload is embarrassingly parallel and interruption-tolerant. This is where the largest growth line lands.
AI
Vertex AI and Model Garden let the agent fleet run on managed infrastructure alongside the data it operates on, with the option to route across model families rather than commit to one. Today that fleet runs entirely off Google Cloud.
Consolidation
One provider for storage, compute, inference and CI removes cross-cloud egress and the operational overhead of a split estate. For a small team running regulated infrastructure, that simplification has real value.
Control and residency
A regulated firm cannot run on the cheapest available compute. IAM and least-privilege access, per-client data isolation, encrypted key custody, controlled deployment pipelines, audit trails and regional data-residency controls are requirements, not preferences — and they are a large part of why this is a Google Cloud decision rather than a GPU-price decision.
Longer term
If DART scales as intended, Google's consumption grows with our institutional user base rather than with a fixed research budget. That is a different and better shape of customer than a one-off workload.

What it costs, and what it would cost here

This volume runs today on flat-fee subscription products, entirely off Google Cloud, at approximately $1,500 a month. Priced at standard metered rates the same volume would be far higher. The difference is a function of subscription pricing and of the 98.3% cache-read share, not of anything clever.

We carry this line in the forecast at $3,000 rising to $4,000 a month, which is what we would realistically expect to pay to run it on Vertex AI at metered rates, with the fleet growing across the window. That is the one line in the model reflecting intent rather than measurement: the volume underneath it is measured precisely; the price we would pay for it is a negotiation.

We flag it plainly because it is the material fact of the migration. This workload is not on anyone's cloud bill today. Moving it to Vertex AI is net-new consumption for Google, and it is the single largest thing a credit would unlock.

Appendix B

Research and simulation unit economics

The research line grows faster than anything else in the forecast because three quantities multiply. This is the arithmetic.

InputTodayMonth 12Note
Strategy archetypes~10~20 Each archetype runs ten backtests a day.
Backtests per month~3,000~6,000 Plus one T+1 reconciliation run per live archetype per day.
Cost per backtest, narrow universe$0.60$0.60 About 3 vCPU-hours, blended across Spot and Cloud Run.
Cost per backtest, full universe$8.50$8.50 About 100 vCPU-hours. Crypto and traditional markets move to full over the window.

Universe width is the sensitive variable. The same research programme costs roughly fourteen times more against the full captured corpus than against the narrow sample. That is not waste — it is the difference between a strategy validated on a slice and one validated on everything we hold, which is precisely what an institutional allocator asks about. It is also why the research line dominates the growth curve, and why a budget constraint shows up first as a decision to test on less data.

Appendix D

Scenario model

The base case is the committed plan. $300,000 in year one — $20,700 a month rising to $28,100 — is what we commit to and what the taper is priced on. It is not a floor beneath some other forecast; it is the forecast. Ambitious is the same roadmap unconstrained, and is upside for Google rather than a gap we are failing to fill. No arrangement is what happens if we cannot make the economics work.

Table view

September 2026 to August 2027. Base is the committed plan of section 4, deliberately paced to what we can fund at $5,000 a month net — live trading from October, research widening steadily, the agent fleet migrating onto Vertex. Ambitious is the same roadmap unconstrained: the full captured universe across all asset groups, more mandates, accelerators in continuous use. The difference between those two is not scope, it is when. No arrangement is a different thing entirely — not a slower roadmap but a smaller Google Cloud: we hold at roughly what we can self- fund and migrate compute, inference and CI to AWS and Azure, leaving bulk storage behind.

How this reconciles with the three-year taper Base and ambitious are not competing forecasts — they are the same curve at two speeds. The ambitious case reaches within twelve months a scale the committed plan does not reach until year two ($780k against the plan’s $600k in year two). Funding does not change what we build; it changes how quickly Google’s consumption arrives — and under the taper, arriving sooner means Odum paying a larger share of it sooner.

The third line is the one worth sitting with. Without an arrangement, Google Cloud consumption over the same twelve months is roughly $33,000 — about 11% of the committed case, and still falling at the end of it. That is not us bluffing about leaving; it is the arithmetic of a firm that cannot carry $25,000 a month gross unsupported and would keep only the part that is genuinely hard to move.

Appendix E

Method, sources and where we might be wrong

Sources

  • Google Cloud spend — BigQuery billing export, table gcp_billing_export_v1_016B25_109840_AF2ACB, 1 May to 9 August 2026. The account settles in GBP; USD figures are computed per line as cost divided by that row's conversion rate, not converted at a period average.
  • AWS spend — Cost Explorer, payer account, unblended monthly.
  • Storage estate — Cloud Monitoring per-bucket total bytes.
  • Agent fleet volume — per-request usage records across seven days of agent transcripts.

Where we might be wrong

  • The March to April figure is an estimate, not the export. The billing export was enabled on 30 April 2026 and is not retroactive. The roughly $30,000 gross for 9 March to 30 April is reconstructed from console recollection. If a certified figure is required it must come from the Cloud Billing console. Everything from 1 May is measured line by line.
  • Per-backtest cost is modelled bottom-up, not benchmarked. We have not yet run a full-pool backtest end to end, so this is the least-verified unit cost in the model — and it multiplies by thousands of runs.
  • The live-trading line is sized from the always-on service footprint, because we have not yet run a strategy in production. It is the line most likely to be wrong in either direction.
  • The agent-fleet price is a judgement; the volume is not. We carry the line at roughly twice today's subscription cost because that is what we would expect to pay to move. A different negotiated rate moves that line directly.
  • One artefact bucket holds 51 TB of deployment tarballs accumulating without a lifecycle rule. That is a cleanup we owe ourselves, and it makes the storage line slightly pessimistic.
  • The schedule assumes slippage. Our internal roadmap had live trading starting in September; we have moved it to October because we are behind, and shifted the rest to match. If we recover that time, the curve arrives earlier.
