## Slide 2 — The Logic

**"Why Breadth Exists"**

We can't build and scale every strategy ourselves. We don't have the manpower, the capital, or the distribution network
to do that alone. And we don't need to.

The breadth we've built serves three purposes. First, it powers our investment management service — we pick strategies,
we trade our own capital, and we partition away our alpha. That alpha stays internal. We don't show it to anyone.

Second, it lets us build bespoke white-label solutions for clients who want access to the same underlying infrastructure
we trade on ourselves — but with their own strategies, their own risk profiles, their own commercial terms. We built
this for ourselves first. It was good enough for our own capital, so it should be good enough for yours. That's the
trust proposition.

Third, signal leasing. Our research produces strategy signals that have commercial value beyond our own trading. We can
lease those signals to institutional counterparties — a major quantitative trading firm and a second institutional
counterparty — we are already in active conversations with both — without sharing any methodology or intellectual
property. They get the signals. They never see how we generate them.

Critically — we will never front-run a client or build strategies that overlap with what we've built for them. We have
clients for every service we offer. Trust is established through the fact that we use this ourselves, we vet everything
to the same standard, and nothing goes through that we wouldn't trust with our own money.

The investment management side grows through track record. The platform side grows through client deployments. Signal
leasing monetises our research without sharing methodology. All three run on the same foundation.

## Slide 6 — Alpha Partitioning

**"What We Keep, What We Share"**

This is important for anyone who asks about intellectual property.

We partition our strategies into two categories.

Internal alpha — strategies we run for our own investment management business. These are proprietary. We don't share the
signal logic, the feature weights, or the specific parameters. Clients in our investment management service get exposure
to the returns, not the methodology.

Bespoke client strategies — strategies we build for platform clients. These are developed on the same infrastructure but
with the client's requirements, risk profiles, and commercial terms. We don't run the client's strategy ourselves. If
you ask us to build a decentralised finance lending strategy for your fund, we build it, you run it, and we don't trade
it alongside you.

The infrastructure is shared. The alpha is not.

This is why breadth matters — we have enough strategy families that we can allocate some to internal investment
management and still have plenty of capacity to build bespoke solutions for clients without any conflict of interest.

## Slide 7 — Why Clients Don't Have to Share Alpha

**"You Don't Have to Give Us Anything"**

This question comes up a lot: why would someone want to work with a firm that also trades?

The answer is you don't have to share anything. The platform is modular. You can engage at whatever level you're
comfortable with.

Just data — you get normalised feeds across all five asset classes. We never see your signals.

Just execution — you plug your own signals into our execution layer. We route and fill. We don't see the logic behind
your orders.

Just reporting — you use our client reporting and compliance tools. Your strategy stays entirely yours.

Just research — you use our backtesting environment. When you're done, you take your results and execute elsewhere if
you want.

The full platform — you run everything on our infrastructure. Even then, we make bespoke commercial deals. We don't
trade the same strategy you're running. We don't need to — we have our own.

The key point is that the engagement is modular and the commercial terms are bespoke. We're not asking anyone to hand
over their edge.

## Slide 8 — Why This Needs Experienced Humans

**"Why Can't Someone Just Do This With AI?"**

People ask: if you're using AI-assisted development, why can't someone just replicate this with AI alone?

Three reasons.

First, we had decades of experience at large trading institutions making money before Odum even existed. I've personally
traded options, delta one, high frequency, medium frequency across traditional finance, crypto, and sports. That
experience shapes what gets built and how. AI is a force multiplier, but it multiplies the judgement of the person
directing it. If you don't know what a good fill looks like, AI won't tell you.

Second, accountability when things go wrong. If you have a significant drawdown and your entire system was built by AI
without deep understanding, you can't debug it under pressure. You want to be able to switch off the AI and solve things
yourself if you need to. That requires a team that understands the code, the strategies, and the markets — not just the
prompts.

Third, the gap between building and operating. AI can help build software quickly. Operating a live trading system
across five asset classes, managing real capital, handling venue outages, reconciling positions across chains — that's
operational experience. It compounds over years. You can't prompt-engineer your way to it.

We use AI heavily in development and operations — and believe anyone should. But the system works because there are
experienced humans, with decades at large trading institutions, making the critical decisions.
